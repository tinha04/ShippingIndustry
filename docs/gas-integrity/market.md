## 1. Problem (V)

- Fixed gas sensors drift and get tested with the wrong gas; inspectors find it, and ships face delays, deficiencies or detention [1 to 3].
- Gas carriers are detained more often than average in at least one US sector (5% against 1.5%) [4].
- The record counts: an undocumented calibration is treated as no calibration [7]; vetting checks the same instruments [8].
- New fuels raise the stakes: ammonia alarms start at 25 ppm, it is toxic, and ports are drawing toxic safety zones [9, 10, 20].

## 2. Why now (V)

The fleet that must run fixed gas detection is growing fast: 942 LNG capable ships in operation and 705 on order; 159 methanol or ethanol capable ships and 293 on order; the first ammonia ships delivered in 2026 [13 to 15, 18]. Alternative fuel orders are up 53% in 2026 [16]. Ammonia refueling trials start in Singapore and Hamburg [19]. Fuel records are going digital (Singapore e-BDN, Amsterdam Rotterdam Antwerp mass flow meters) [21], so a digital safety record fits the direction ports are already moving.

## 4. Buyers

| Buyer | Why they pay | Concentration |
|---|---|---|
| Ship managers (technical) | Avoid detentions and delays; less crew time; one view across makers | Medium: large managers run hundreds of ships |
| Owners of gas carriers and gas fueled ships | Charter and vetting performance; crew safety | Medium |
| Charterers and terminals | Want proof before berthing (through vetting) | High |
| Insurers | Data on new fuel risks [12] | High |
| Gas detection makers | A neutral record could be a feature they license | High: few makers |

## 5. Read on venture scale

Tens of millions a year from gas detection alone. A $100M+ path needs tankers and other ships, land sites, or the nuclear step (radiation monitors, then reactor monitoring). Its role here is a business that earns now and proves our record at sea.

## 6. Assumptions to test

1. Ship managers pay per ship for proof and drift warnings, not only for the maker's service.
2. Panels from the main makers expose usable data without the maker's help.
3. Class accepts a read only tap without a full new approval.
4. Inspectors and vetting accept a digital signed record.
5. A detention or delay from gas detection happens often enough to justify the price (needs newer port state control data).
