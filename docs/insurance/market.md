## 1. Problem (V)

- Nuclear risks are excluded from almost all marine policies [3] and from the shipowner insurers' shared reinsurance [1]. NorthStandard can offer only $50M for nuclear claims [2].
- No liability treaty is in force [4]; the IMO legal working group asked to start one in January 2026 [5]. Without it, cover could be "unaffordable" [6].
- Insurers say the products don't exist yet [7]. MARAD asked industry for insurance pathways [L1].

## 2. Why now (V)

AXA XL joined NEMO (February 2026) [10]; Markel and Willis opened a nuclear facility (May 2026) [11]; Marsh placed cover for TerraPower's plant [15]; NorthStandard works on insurance pathways in the Lloyd's Register consortium [16]; NEMO's insurability guide is due 2027 [17].

## 3. Bottom up (E)

| Path | Assumption | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| A. Risk data | 20 insurers, brokers and projects x $50K to $200K a year | $1M to $4M a year |
| B. Port refusal or delay cover | 50 ships x $200K to $500K premium a year x 20% to 30% agency commission | $2M to $7.5M a year |
| C. Nuclear layers as an underwriting agency | 50 ships x $2M to $5M nuclear premium a year x 20% to 30% commission | $20M to $75M a year |
| D. Floating plants | 20 units, same structure as C at half the premium | $4M to $15M a year |
| **Total at 50 ships** | | **About $25M to $100M a year**, mostly path C after a treaty or backstop |

**Overfitting warning:** the premium per ship has no market quote; it is a guess from the size of the liability gap. Path C needs a license, capital and a legal framework that do not exist yet.

## 4. Buyers

| Buyer | Why they pay | Concentration |
|---|---|---|
| Insurers and underwriting agencies (Northcourt, AXA XL, Markel) | Price a new class of risk without building the data themselves | High: few players |
| Brokers (Marsh, Willis) | Place cover for their clients | High |
| Shipowners and floating plant developers | No cover, no voyage | Low today |
| Ports | Proof of cover before a call | Medium |

## 5. Read on venture scale

Potentially the largest of the three, but the furthest away and the hardest to enter. Paths A and B are small; path C needs years of law and capital. Strongest as stage 3 of one company that already holds port and operating records ([portfolio](../portfolio.md#2-how-the-three-ideas-fit-proposal)).

## 6. Assumptions to test

1. An insurer will pay for risk data before any nuclear ship exists.
2. A capital partner will back port refusal cover.
3. Shipowners see port refusal as a real cost worth insuring.
4. A treaty or national backstop arrives before about 2030.
