## 2. Our gap

1. **Ship specific data:** port readiness per route (idea 1) and an independent operating record (idea 2). Incumbents price plants that stay in one place.
2. **Port refusal cover:** a commercial risk no product covers (none found, H), priced from data we hold.
3. **Neutral:** not tied to a reactor maker, class society or insurer.

## 3. Where we lose

- Northcourt, AXA XL or NorthStandard builds the ship product with the Lloyd's Register consortium [16].
- No capital partner works with a startup.
- A treaty or backstop is years away, so there is nothing to insure.
- Underwriters build their own risk models and don't buy ours.
- A single port event (radiation release) makes all nuclear ship cover unavailable.

## 4. Strategy

Sell data to the incumbents first and become their partner, not their rival. Test port refusal cover with one capital partner. Hire a nuclear underwriter before asking for capacity. Become an underwriting agency only when a capital partner commits.

## 5. Stress test: new, or better? (2026-10-07)

**Improved, not new, and the weakest position.** Nuclear underwriting exists (Northcourt, pools, Markel and Willis) [11 to 14]; big insurers and brokers are entering [10, 15]; the Lloyd's Register consortium has an insurer [16]. No ship focused startup found [19]. What stays open: ship and route specific risk data, and port refusal cover. **Value lever to test:** whether insurers pay for data before ships exist.
