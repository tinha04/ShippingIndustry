## 1. Problem (V)

- A floating or ship reactor moves between jurisdictions. Who inspects it, and how, is "still being studied" [3]; monitors and seals stay necessary even with satellite imagery [2].
- Ports must plan emergencies and security for each call [L6]; insurers need data to price cover (idea 3). Today the reactor maker holds the data.
- Ships are frequent cyber targets [4], and a nuclear ship falls under two cyber rulebooks at once [5, 6].

## 2. Why now (V)

NRC proposed Part 57 (final expected January 2027) allows remote monitoring and operation [1, L16]; the IAEA program has a safeguards group [L14]; ship cyber rules apply to new ships [5]; floating plant money arrived in 2026 [L7, L8].

## 4. Buyers

| Buyer | Why they pay | Concentration |
|---|---|---|
| Floating plant developers | Port and regulator acceptance; insurer data | Medium: about 6 developers [L7, L8, L12] |
| Ship operators | Port entry, insurance, regulator oversight | Low today; grows with fleets |
| Insurers | Priced cover needs an operating record | High |
| Regulators and the IAEA | Oversight of moving reactors | Few; may set requirements rather than pay |

## 5. Read on venture scale

Tens of millions a year from marine reactors alone; $100M+ needs a large fleet or land microreactors. Strongest as stage 2 of one company that already holds the port records ([portfolio](../portfolio.md#2-how-the-three-ideas-fit-proposal)).

## 6. Assumptions to test

1. Reactor developers will share read only data with a third party.
2. Regulators, ports or insurers will accept or require independent records.
3. Floating plant developers will pilot before their first fueled unit.
4. The price per reactor covers nuclear grade quality costs.
