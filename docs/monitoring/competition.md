## 2. Our gap

1. **Independent:** the only record not owned by the reactor maker or operator (none found [15]).
2. **Cross border by design:** offline first, signed, shared by role, so a port in another country can trust it.
3. **One record for four readers:** regulator, port, insurer and IAEA, instead of four reports.
4. **Linked to port readiness:** the pre arrival packet lands in the port's file (idea 1).

## 3. Where we lose

- Reactor makers bundle monitoring and refuse third party access.
- Regulators accept only the licensee's own systems.
- Mirion or Kongsberg adds a shared nuclear record to products ships already buy.
- Nuclear grade quality and cyber certification outlast our runway.
- The first fueled reactor at sea slips past 2030.

## 4. Strategy

Partner with detector makers (Mirion) and cyber firms (Cydome, Xage) rather than compete. Pilot on a floating plant prototype first. Sell the record, not the sensors. Ask insurers and ports to name independent monitoring as a condition; that is what makes makers open their data.

## 5. Stress test: new, or better? (2026-10-07)

**Improved, not new.** Radiation monitoring, ship remote operations, ship and reactor cyber security, and plant AI all exist [9 to 14]. No independent, cross vendor record for moving reactors found [15]. What stays open: a neutral record that a foreign port, an insurer and the IAEA can all trust. **Value lever to test:** whether insurers or ports will require it, which is what would make reactor makers share data.
